About Virginia

Meaningful growth with service at the center.

Virginia is a Licensed Professional Counselor who has worked with clients experiencing depression, anxiety, relationship issues, parenting concerns, self-esteem concerns, grief, physical trauma, emotional abuse, and sexual abuse.

"To Live is To Serve." Frances Hesselbein

Education and early community work

In 2010, Virginia earned her bachelor's degree from the University of Alabama with a major in Psychology and a minor in Anthropology. Her commitment to serving adolescents began during college in 2008 when she served as a teaching assistant for Psychology 450, planning civic youth engagement events with at-risk high school students.

During the summer of 2009 in San Pedro Sula, Honduras, Virginia completed an independent study as primary investigator on self-esteem and family moral-religious emphasis among female adolescent Honduran orphans. Her research was published as a poster and presented at the 2010 Southeastern Psychological Association convention in Chattanooga.

Following college, Virginia volunteered at a Southeast Asian nonprofit in Bayou La Batre, Alabama, where she founded a youth empowerment program for middle and upper school students from 2010 to 2012. With support from administrators at Boat People SOS, she wrote a grant that created a paid role and facilitated teenage leadership groups. Their projects included park beautification with City Council approval, a bullying prevention campaign supported by Mobile District Attorney Ashley Rich, and a community carnival intended to lift morale following natural disasters including Hurricane Katrina. Virginia treasures receiving the name "Ai Vi" and the honor of being recognized as a Vietnamese American Leader after a year of training through the BPSOS 500 in 5 Years Pilot Project.

Clinical and public behavioral health experience

Virginia earned her master's degree in Clinical Mental Health Counseling from the University of Alabama at Birmingham in 2014. She has worked in adolescent residential, outpatient, school-based, and private practice settings, serving children, adolescents, adults, and couples. She has also witnessed the meaningful progress clients can make through virtual video-based therapy since the onset of COVID-19.

In 2016, Virginia helped develop the Eagle Rock Boys Ranch Mentoring Program in Etowah County, matching professional adult volunteers with at-risk youth needing support. The program, later known as APEX, continued to serve boys and adult mentors as of 2023.

While serving for more than two years as Clinical Behavioral Health Administrator at the Mobile County Health Department from 2018 to 2020, Virginia helped create and host more than 42 videos for the Wellness Wednesday with Virginia series. She also revised the health department's suicide policy in partnership with the psychology department at the University of South Alabama, developed practical suicide-prevention resources for primary care providers within their electronic medical record, delivered face-to-face training for more than 500 staff members, and developed suicide prevention video tutorials for staff.

During the onset of COVID-19, Virginia addressed the Mobile community virtually with behavioral health guidance for navigating the pandemic; her messages were broadcast by local news sources including Fox 10 News. In 2020, she also led a public forum with leaders from varied ethnic communities to address civil unrest in the nation.

What therapy is like with Virginia

Virginia primarily uses Motivational Interviewing, positive psychology, and Cognitive Behavioral Therapy to guide clients toward meaningful growth. She values a trusting therapeutic alliance and recognizes the importance of communication with caregivers when supporting young people.

Service and professional affiliations

Virginia's service roles include Lifelines Rape Victim Advocate, Assistant Scout Master of Boy Scout Troop 505, and Father/Daughter Mentor and Coach for Outback of America.

She is a member of the American Counseling Association and the Alabama Counseling Association and a former board member of the Mobile Area Council, Boy Scouts of America. Virginia is also recognized by the American Society for Reproductive Medicine as a certified mental health professional providing care to infertility patients.

Media and resources

In 2022, Virginia collaborated with a former colleague from the Mobile County Health Department to launch TeenPOD on Spotify and Apple platforms. Through conversations with successful leaders and mentors, the podcast explores how people overcame adversity during their teen years. Her TeenPOD website serves as an additional hub of resources for teens and caregivers.
